# English comparatives (example)

- Short adjectives add -er/-est (tall, taller, tallest).
- Long adjectives use more/most (beautiful, more beautiful).
- Good/well, bad/badly, far form irregularly: better/best, worse/worst, farther/farthest.
- Than introduces the compared item: taller than her brother.
- Double comparatives (more taller) are nonstandard in formal English.
- Absolute adjectives (unique, perfect, dead) resist grading logically.
- Less/least invert uncountables where fewer/fewest count items.
- As...as frames equality: as fast as light.
- The-comparative pairs track linked changes: the more, the merrier.
- Superlatives need the and a defined comparison set (tallest in class).
