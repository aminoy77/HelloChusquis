# English plurals (example)

- Most nouns add -s (cats) and sibilants add -es (buses, boxes).
- Consonant-plus-y becomes -ies (city/cities); vowel-plus-y just adds -s (boys).
- F/fe endings often become -ves (leaf/leaves, knife/knives) with exceptions.
- Latin -us/-a/-um nouns vary: cacti, phenomena, data (plural of datum).
- Greek -on becomes -a in phenomena and criteria.
- Unchanging nouns include sheep, deer, fish (one species), and series.
- Always-plural nouns (scissors, trousers, thanks) take plural verbs.
- Compound nouns pluralize the head word: mothers-in-law, passersby.
- Zero plurals cover game animals (elk, moose) and some fish.
- Foreign plurals coexist with anglicized forms (indexes/indices) by register.
