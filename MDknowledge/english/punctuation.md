# English punctuation (example)

- Commas join independent clauses only with conjunctions (and, but, so).
- Semicolons link related independent clauses without conjunctions.
- Colons introduce lists, explanations, and quotations after complete sentences.
- Apostrophes mark possession (dog's) and contractions (don't), never plurals.
- Quotation punctuation sits inside quotes in US style and logically in UK style.
- Em dashes insert dramatic asides — stronger than commas, looser than colons.
- Hyphens join compound modifiers before nouns (well-known actor).
- Parentheses de-emphasize extras; dashes emphasize them; commas stay neutral.
- Question marks close direct questions only, not indirect ones (She asked why.).
- Serial (Oxford) commas prevent list ambiguities in formal writing.
