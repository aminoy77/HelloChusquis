# Presentations (example)

- Open with a story, statistic, or question — never an agenda slide.
- One idea per slide keeps audiences following instead of reading ahead.
- Rule of three structures messages into memorable triads.
- Images beat bullet walls; speak the details slides omit.
- Rehearse aloud with timing; silent run-throughs hide pacing problems.
- End with a clear call to action, not a thank-you slide.
- Q&A preparation lists the ten hardest questions with crisp answers.
- Eye contact plus pauses project confidence more than slides do.
- Backup PDFs survive projector and font failures gracefully.
- Record rehearsals to catch filler words and rushed sections.
