# Geneva Conventions (example)

- Four 1949 treaties plus protocols codify wartime humanitarian law.
- Protect wounded, shipwrecked, prisoners, and civilians categorically.
- Common Article 3 sets minimums even in non-international conflicts.
- Grave breaches (torture, hostage-taking) trigger universal jurisdiction.
- ICRC guardianship (visits, tracing) operationalizes the treaties neutrally.
- Additional Protocols (1977) cover civil wars and indiscriminate weapons.
- Ratification is near-universal (196 states), uniquely complete.
- War-crimes tribunals (Yugoslavia, Rwanda, ICC) enforce selectively.
- Medical emblems (cross, crescent, crystal) protect marked personnel.
- Cyber-warfare interpretations (Tallinn Manual) extend old rules uneasily.
