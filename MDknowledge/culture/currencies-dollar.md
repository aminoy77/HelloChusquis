# US dollar (example)

- The dollar ($) divides into 100 cents with coins to $1 and bills to $100.
- Federal Reserve notes all read 'legal tender for all debts'.
- Dollar dominance prices oil, settles trade, and anchors reserves globally.
- Bretton Woods (1944) pegged currencies to dollar-convertible gold.
- Nixon Shock (1971) floated the dollar, ending gold convertibility.
- Petrodollar recycling ties oil trade to dollar demand structurally.
- $100 bills circulate mostly abroad, often informally or illicitly.
- Coin compositions shifted copper-to-zinc and silver-to-clad historically.
- Strong-dollar cycles squeeze emerging-market dollar borrowers.
- Digital dollar debates weigh CBDC design against private stablecoins.
