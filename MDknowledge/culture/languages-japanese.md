# Japanese language (example)

- Japanese counts ~125M speakers almost entirely in Japan.
- Three scripts mix: kanji (meaning), hiragana (grammar), katakana (foreign).
- Pitch accent (not stress) distinguishes words like hashi subtly.
- Keigo honorifics calibrate politeness across social hierarchies elaborately.
- Topic marker wa versus subject ga encodes given-versus-new information.
- Verbs come last; adjectives inflect for tense like verbs do.
- Counters (hon, mai, tsu) classify counted objects obligatorily.
- Onomatopoeia dictionaries (giongo/gitaigo) run thousands deep.
- Borrowed English (wasei-eigo) often means something different locally.
- JLPT levels (N5-N1) gate study, work, and visa opportunities.
