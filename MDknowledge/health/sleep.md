# Sleep science (example)

- Adults need 7-9 hours; athletes and teens need more, not less.
- 90-minute cycles alternate deep (body) and REM (brain) sleep functions.
- Consistent wake times anchor circadian rhythms more than bedtimes.
- Morning daylight (10+ minutes) sets melatonin timing powerfully.
- Caffeine's half-life (~6 hours) means afternoon cups steal night sleep.
- Alcohol fragments REM despite hastening sleep onset deceptively.
- Cool (~18°C), dark, quiet rooms optimize sleep architecture cheaply.
- Screens' blue light delays melatonin; night modes help marginally.
- Chronic short sleep impairs cognition like legal drunkenness measurably.
- Naps (20 min or 90 min) avoid groggy mid-cycle awakenings.
