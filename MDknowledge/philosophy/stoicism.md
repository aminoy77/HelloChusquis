# Stoicism (example)

- Stoicism (Zeno, ~300 BC) trains distinguishing controllable from uncontrollable.
- Dichotomy of control: judgments and actions are yours; outcomes are not.
- Marcus Aurelius's Meditations journals Stoic practice as Roman emperor.
- Seneca's letters apply Stoicism to wealth, anger, and time use.
- Epictetus (ex-slave) taught freedom as internal assent, not circumstance.
- Negative visualization (premeditatio malorum) inoculates against fortune.
- Amor fati (love of fate) converts obstacles into training material.
- Virtue (wisdom, courage, justice, temperance) is the sole true good.
- Journaling evening reviews (what done well/poorly) structures practice.
- Modern CBT descends directly from Stoic thought-restructuring techniques.
