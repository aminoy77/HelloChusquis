# Socrates (example)

- Socrates (470-399 BC) wrote nothing; Plato's dialogues preserve his voice.
- Socratic irony professed ignorance to expose interlocutors' confusion.
- Elenchus cross-examination refuted definitions through counterexamples.
- 'The unexamined life is not worth living' anchors his Apology defense.
- Athens executed him for impiety and corrupting youth in 399 BC.
- Hemlock death scene (Phaedo) stages philosophy facing mortality calmly.
- Socratic method still structures law-school cold calls today.
- Virtue-as-knowledge claims wrongdoing stems from ignorance, not malice.
- His daimonion (inner voice) warned against actions, never commanded.
- Street philosophizing rejected fees, unlike Sophist contemporaries.
