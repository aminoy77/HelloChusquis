# Modular arithmetic (example)

- a ≡ b (mod n) means n divides their difference exactly.
- Clock arithmetic (12 ≡ 0 mod 12) models cycles intuitively.
- Addition/multiplication carry through congruences componentwise safely.
- Modular inverses (a⁻¹ with a·a⁻¹ ≡ 1) exist exactly when coprime to n.
- Extended Euclid computes inverses constructively for cryptography.
- Fermat/Euler theorems shrink exponents in modular powers drastically.
- Chinese remainder combines coprime-modulus systems into single solutions.
- Parity, divisibility rules, and check digits are everyday modular uses.
- Diffie-Hellman key exchange rides discrete-log hardness modulo primes.
- Calendar/day-of-week math is modular arithmetic in disguise.
