# Number theory (example)

- Primes have exactly two divisors; composites factor uniquely (fundamental theorem).
- Euclid's algorithm finds GCDs in logarithmic time via remainders.
- Sieve of Eratosthenes marks composites to list primes efficiently.
- There are infinitely many primes (Euclid's classic contradiction proof).
- Modular arithmetic wraps numbers (`15 ≡ 1 mod 7`) powering cryptography.
- Fermat's little theorem (a^(p−1) ≡ 1 mod p) underlies primality tests.
- RSA multiplies large primes; factoring difficulty secures the internet.
- Diophantine equations seek integer solutions (Pythagorean triples famously).
- Twin primes (p, p+2) conjecturally infinite but unproven.
- Perfect numbers equal their proper-divisor sums (6, 28, 496...).
