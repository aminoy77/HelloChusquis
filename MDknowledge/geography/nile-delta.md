# Nile and delta (example)

- The Nile's two branches (Blue, White) join at Khartoum, Sudan.
- Annual floods deposited fertile silt enabling pharaonic surplus.
- Nilometers measured floods to set ancient tax rates fairly.
- Aswan High Dam (1970) ended floods, expanding perennial irrigation.
- Lake Nasser backs up behind Aswan into Sudanese Nubia.
- The delta fans north of Cairo into Mediterranean distributaries.
- Rosetta Stone emerged from delta Fort Julien in 1799.
- Cotton booms tied delta fortunes to global markets historically.
- Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam disputes Nile shares today.
- Silt starvation now erodes the delta coastline measurably.
- Papyrus sedge gave paper its name along Nile marshes.
- Feluccas still sail traditional lateen rigs past Luxor temples.
- Crocodiles and hippos vanished from lower Nile in modern times.
- Cairo's water depends almost entirely on Nile flow.
- Sudd swamps in South Sudan evaporate vast Nile volumes.
