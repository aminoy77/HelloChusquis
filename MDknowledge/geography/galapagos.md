# Galapagos (example)

- Galapagos volcanic islands straddle the equator 900 km off Ecuador.
- Darwin's finches showcased adaptive radiation to the Beagle visitor.
- Giant tortoises live over a century and rebounded via breeding centers.
- Marine iguanas are the world's only seagoing lizards.
- Blue-footed boobies dance their feet in mating displays.
- Flightless cormorants traded wings for diving prowess.
- Strict visitor rules (guides, paths, distance) protect tame wildlife.
- El Niño events crash marine food webs periodically.
- Pirates and whalers stocked tortoises as living ship provisions.
- UNESCO listed the islands in 1978 among first World Heritage sites.
- Lava tunnels and craters record hotspot volcanism visibly.
- Invasive goats/rats required major eradication campaigns.
- Currents (Humboldt, Cromwell) upwell nutrients feeding penguins at the equator.
- Island-hopping cruises fund conservation through park fees.
- Each island's mockingbirds differ, inspiring Darwin alongside finches.
