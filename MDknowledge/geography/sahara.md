# Sahara desert (example)

- The Sahara is the largest hot desert at about 9 million square kilometers.
- Sahara dunes (ergs) cover a minority; most is gravel plains (regs) and rock.
- Day-night temperature swings exceed 30°C regularly in the deep Sahara.
- The Nile and Niger rivers water its southern and eastern fringes.
- Tuareg nomads navigate the central Sahara by camel caravans traditionally.
- Trans-Saharan trade once moved gold and salt between kingdoms.
- Rock art depicts a greener Sahara with lakes ~10,000 years ago.
- Sand seas like the Grand Erg Oriental shift dunes tens of meters yearly.
- Oases (Siwa, Timimoun) anchor date farming amid hyper-aridity.
- Desertification debates track the Sahel's moving southern boundary.
- Acacia and drought grasses survive on rare, violent downpours.
- Fennec foxes radiate heat through oversized ears at night.
- Solar mega-projects eye Sahara irradiance for European power.
- Sandstorms (haboobs) can ground flights across North Africa.
- Timbuktu's libraries preserved Saharan scholarship through centuries.
