# Niagara Falls (example)

- Niagara straddles the US-Canada border between Erie and Ontario lakes.
- Horseshoe Falls (Canadian side) carries most of the flow.
- Goat Island divides American and Bridal Veil falls on the US side.
- Flow peaks daytime for power generation, restored nightly for views.
- Maid of the Mist boats sail into the Horseshoe's spray since 1846.
- Tightrope walkers (Blondin) and barrel riders (Taylor) stunt its history.
- Hydro plants on both sides share the river's power by treaty.
- Erosion once retreated the falls meters yearly; controls slowed it.
- Winter ice bridges sometimes jam the gorge spectacularly.
- Nightly illumination colors the cascades for evening crowds.
- Welland Canal bypasses the falls for Great Lakes shipping.
- Devil's Hole rapids downstream rank among Class V+ whitewater.
- Table Rock viewpoint crowds the Horseshoe's brink in Ontario.
- Indigenous portage routes long predated European 'discovery'.
- Honeymoon capital branding dates to 19th-century rail tourism.
