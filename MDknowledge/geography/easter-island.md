# Easter Island (example)

- Rapa Nui (Easter Island) lies ~3,500 km west of Chile in the Pacific.
- Nearly 900 moai statues gaze inland from coastal platforms (ahu).
- Rano Raraku quarry still holds half-buried unfinished moai.
- Deforestation plus rats likely doomed the island's palm forests.
- Birdman (Tangata Manu) rites succeeded ancestor cults by the 1700s.
- Rongorongo script remains undeciphered among few wooden tablets.
- Chile annexed the island in 1888; islanders gained full citizenship in 1966.
- Sheep ranching confined Rapa Nui people to Hanga Roa for decades.
- Tapati festival revives traditional sports and arts each February.
- Tourism now funds preservation amid carrying-capacity debates.
- Poike legends recall 'long ears' versus 'short ears' conflicts.
- Sweet potatoes link Rapa Nui to South American contact theories.
- Orongo village perches dramatically on Rano Kau crater rim.
- Moai hats (pukao) likely rolled up ramps with rope techniques.
- Dark skies earned the island stargazing tourism fame.
