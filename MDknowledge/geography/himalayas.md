# Himalayas (example)

- The Himalayas hold all fourteen 8,000-meter peaks on Earth.
- Everest grows millimeters yearly as India pushes into Asia tectonically.
- Monsoon snows feed Asia's great rivers (Ganges, Yangtze, Mekong).
- Sherpa communities enable high-altitude climbing logistics.
- Yeti legends persist in Himalayan folklore and footprints.
- Bhutan measures peaks sacred and bans mountaineering above 6,000 m.
- Annapurna Circuit treks cross 5,416-meter Thorong La pass.
- High-altitude pulmonary/cerebral edema kills the unacclimatized fast.
- Yarsagumba fungus harvests fund remote Nepali villages seasonally.
- Glacial lake outburst floods threaten valleys as ice melts.
- Red pandas inhabit eastern Himalayan bamboo forests.
- Tibetan Buddhism's monasteries (Tengboche) anchor Sherpa culture.
- First Everest summit (1953): Hillary and Norgay via South Col.
- K2's remoteness keeps it wilder than crowded Everest.
- Siachen Glacier hosts the world's highest battlefield.
