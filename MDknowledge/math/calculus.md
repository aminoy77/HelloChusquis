# Calculus essentials

- In calculus, the derivative of x squared is 2x by the power rule.
- In calculus, the derivative measures instantaneous rate of change as the limit of secant slopes.
- In calculus, the power rule drops exponents: d/dx[x^n] = n·x^(n-1).
- In calculus, the product rule differentiates products: (fg)' = f'g + fg'.
- In calculus, the chain rule differentiates compositions: dy/dx = dy/du · du/dx.
- In calculus, critical points (derivative zero or undefined) locate maxima, minima, and saddles.
- In calculus, the second derivative test classifies critical points by concavity sign.
- In calculus, integrals accumulate: definite integrals measure area under curves.
- In calculus, the fundamental theorem links differentiation and integration as inverse operations.
- In calculus, u-substitution reverses the chain rule for composite integrands.
- In calculus, `e^x` differentiates to itself and `ln x` differentiates to 1/x.
