# Exponents and logarithms

- In math, `x^a · x^b = x^(a+b)` and `(x^a)^b = x^(a·b)` govern exponent arithmetic.
- In math, `x^0 = 1` for nonzero x, and negative exponents reciprocate: `x^-a = 1/x^a`.
- In math, roots are fractional powers: `√x = x^(1/2)` and `∛x = x^(1/3)`.
- In math, logarithms invert exponentials: `log_b(x·y) = log_b(x) + log_b(y)`.
- In math, `log_b(x^k) = k·log_b(x)` pulls powers out front for solving.
- In math, change of base: `log_b(x) = ln(x)/ln(b)` adapts any logarithm.
- In math, exponential growth doubles on fixed schedules; logarithms linearize it for analysis.
- In math, `e ≈ 2.71828` bases natural growth and `ln` inverts it.
- In math, scientific notation `a × 10^n` with 1 ≤ a < 10 tames extreme magnitudes.
- In math, order-of-magnitude estimates use nearest powers of ten before precise computation.
