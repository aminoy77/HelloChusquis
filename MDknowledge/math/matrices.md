# Matrices essentials

- In linear algebra, matrices compose linear maps; multiplication order matters (AB ≠ BA).
- In linear algebra, matrix multiplication needs inner dimensions to match: (m×n)(n×p) gives m×p.
- In linear algebra, the identity matrix leaves every vector unchanged under multiplication.
- In linear algebra, the determinant scales areas/volumes; zero means the map collapses dimensions.
- In linear algebra, invertible matrices have nonzero determinants and solve Ax = b uniquely.
- In linear algebra, the transpose flips rows and columns; symmetric matrices equal their transpose.
- In linear algebra, eigenvectors keep direction under a map, scaled by their eigenvalues.
- In linear algebra, Gaussian elimination row-reduces systems to solve or expose inconsistency.
- In linear algebra, rank counts independent rows/columns and bounds solvable unknowns.
- In linear algebra, dot products measure alignment and projections decompose vectors onto axes.
