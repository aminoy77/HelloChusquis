# Algebra essentials

- Solving means isolating the variable: do the same operation to both sides of an equation.
- Linear equations `ax + b = 0` solve to `x = -b/a` for nonzero a.
- The quadratic formula `x = (-b ± √(b²-4ac)) / 2a` solves `ax² + bx + c = 0`.
- The discriminant `b²-4ac` tells root count: positive two real, zero one repeated, negative two complex.
- Factoring reverses expansion: `x² + 5x + 6 = (x+2)(x+3)`.
- Exponent rules: `x^a · x^b = x^(a+b)`, `(x^a)^b = x^(ab)`, `x^(-a) = 1/x^a`.
- Fractional exponents are roots: `x^(1/2) = √x`, `x^(2/3) = ∛(x²)`.
- Logarithms invert exponentials: `log_b(x·y) = log_b(x) + log_b(y)` and `log_b(x^k) = k·log_b(x)`.
- `ln` is base e and `log` is base 10 by convention in most textbooks and calculators.
- Inequalities flip direction when multiplying or dividing by a negative number.
- Absolute value `|x|` is distance from zero: `|x| < a` means `-a < x < a`.
- Systems of equations solve by substitution or elimination; parallel lines mean no solution.
- Functions map each input to exactly one output; the vertical-line test checks graphs.
- Slope `m = (y₂-y₁)/(x₂-x₁)` measures rate of change; `y = mx + b` is slope-intercept form.
- Polynomials of degree n have at most n real roots (fundamental theorem of algebra).
- Completing the square rewrites `x² + bx` as `(x + b/2)² - (b/2)²` for solving and graphing.
- Extraneous roots appear when squaring both sides; always verify solutions in the original equation.
- Domain restrictions (division by zero, even roots of negatives) must be checked before simplifying.
