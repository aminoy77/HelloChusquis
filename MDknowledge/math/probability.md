# Probability essentials

- In probability, all outcome probabilities sum to exactly 1 across the sample space.
- In probability, P(A and B) = P(A)·P(B) only when events are independent.
- In probability, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B) corrects double-counted overlap.
- In probability, conditional P(A|B) = P(A and B)/P(B) updates beliefs on evidence.
- In probability, Bayes' theorem flips conditionals using base rates: P(H|E) ∝ P(E|H)·P(H).
- In probability, expected value weights outcomes by probability for fair-price reasoning.
- In probability, the gambler's fallacy mistakes independent streaks for balancing forces.
- In probability, combinations C(n,k) count unordered draws and permutations ordered ones.
- In probability, the birthday paradox needs only 23 people for a 50 percent shared birthday.
- In probability, rare events need huge samples before observed rates stabilize.
