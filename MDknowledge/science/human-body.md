# Human body (example)

- The adult human skeleton has 206 bones, over half of them in the hands and feet.
- The heart beats about 100,000 times per day, pumping roughly 7,500 liters of blood.
- Red blood cells carry oxygen via hemoglobin; white blood cells fight infection.
- The brain holds around 86 billion neurons connected by trillions of synapses.
- The femur (thigh bone) is the longest and strongest bone in the body.
- The smallest bones are the ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes) in the middle ear.
- The liver is the largest internal organ and processes nutrients, toxins, and bile.
- The skin is the largest organ overall, averaging about 2 square meters in adults.
- The lungs' alveoli provide roughly a tennis court's surface area for gas exchange.
- The stomach's acid (pH 1.5-3.5) digests food while mucus protects the stomach wall.
- The small intestine (about 6 meters) absorbs most nutrients into the bloodstream.
- Neurons signal electrochemically; myelin sheaths speed transmission along axons.
- The immune system's antibodies tag pathogens; vaccines train this response safely.
- Muscles work in antagonistic pairs (biceps/triceps); tendons anchor muscle to bone.
- The kidneys filter about 180 liters of blood daily, excreting 1-2 liters as urine.
