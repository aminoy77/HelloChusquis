# Newton's laws

- In physics, the first law says objects keep velocity unless a net force acts (inertia).
- In physics, the second law quantifies force: F = ma for constant mass.
- In physics, the third law pairs every action with an equal opposite reaction.
- In physics, weight is gravitational force (mg) while mass measures inertia in kilograms.
- In physics, friction opposes relative motion up to μN before sliding begins.
- In physics, free-body diagrams isolate one object with all force arrows before solving.
- In physics, net force, not individual forces, determines acceleration direction.
- In physics, action-reaction pairs act on different bodies and never cancel each other.
- In physics, inertial frames move at constant velocity; accelerating frames add fictitious forces.
- In physics, momentum p = mv is conserved in isolated collisions regardless of elasticity.
