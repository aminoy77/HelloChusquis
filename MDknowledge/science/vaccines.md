# Vaccines (example)

- Vaccines train immunity using weakened, killed, or partial pathogens.
- Jenner (1796 cowpox) plus Pasteur generalized inoculation scientifically.
- mRNA vaccines deliver instructions; cells manufacture antigens briefly.
- Herd thresholds (measles ~95 percent) shield those who cannot vaccinate.
- Smallpox eradication (1980) remains vaccines' crowning triumph.
- Adjuvants boost responses, enabling smaller antigen doses.
- Cold chains preserve potency from factory to remote clinics critically.
- VAERS-style surveillance catches rare side effects post-approval continuously.
- Placebo-controlled trials quantify efficacy before authorization.
- Hesitancy tracks misinformation exposure measurably in surveys.
