# Seasons (example)

- Seasons come from axial tilt (~23.5°), not varying Earth-Sun distance.
- Solstices mark longest/shortest days; equinoxes equalize them globally.
- Hemispheres oppose: December summer south, winter north.
- Perihelion (January) actually nears Earth to the Sun during northern winter.
- Day length, not just temperature, cues migrations and flowering.
- Tropics see overhead suns twice yearly; poles endure months-long days/nights.
- Monsoons flip winds seasonally, watering South Asia's summers.
- Growing seasons bound agriculture between last and first frosts.
- Climate change shifts phenology (bloom dates, migrations) measurably.
- Milankovitch orbital cycles pace ice ages over tens of millennia.
