# Dystopian fiction (example)

- Dystopias warn by extrapolating present trends to oppressive futures.
- 1984 (surveillance), Brave New World (pleasure-control) frame the debate.
- Atwood's Handmaid's Tale roots horrors in historical precedents deliberately.
- Hunger Games rebooted YA dystopia for 21st-century anxieties.
- Zamyatin's We (1924) pioneered the totalitarian-dystopia template.
- Surveillance plus consumerism fuse in contemporary tech-dystopias.
- Utopian interludes inside dystopias sharpen critique by contrast.
- Cautionary tales risk normalizing the futures they warn against.
- Post-apocalyptic settings reset civilizations to thought experiments.
- Hopeful endings versus bleak ones divide the genre philosophically.
