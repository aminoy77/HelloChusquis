# Cervantes (example)

- Cervantes (1547-1616) wrote Don Quixote, often called the first modern novel.
- Quixote's chivalric delusions collide with innkeepers' mundane reality.
- Sancho Panza's proverbs ground his master's idealism pragmatically.
- Part II (1615) answers a spurious sequel with metafictional games.
- Lepanto battle wounds (1571) cost Cervantes his left hand's use.
- Algerian captivity (1575-80) ransomed after four escape attempts.
- Exemplary Novels pioneered Spanish short fiction between Quixote parts.
- Persiles (posthumous) chased Byzantine romance fashions.
- Quixote's windmill charge emblematizes noble futile striving.
- Unreliable narration plus found-manuscript frames predate postmodernism.
