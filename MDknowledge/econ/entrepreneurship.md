# Entrepreneurship (example)

- Startups search for repeatable models; companies execute known ones.
- Product-market fit (retention without bribery) precedes scaling spend.
- MVPs test riskiest assumptions cheapest before full builds.
- Unit economics (LTV > CAC) must work before growth amplifies anything.
- Bootstrapping keeps control; venture capital buys speed with dilution.
- Runway (cash divided by burn) sets the experiment deadline bluntly.
- Pivots change strategy on validated learning, not on boredom.
- Moats (network effects, switching costs) defend margins from clones.
- Founders sell vision to hires, customers, and investors continuously.
- Most startups die of no market need, not of competitor action.
