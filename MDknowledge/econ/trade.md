# International trade (example)

- Comparative advantage says specialize relatively, then trade, for mutual gain.
- Tariffs tax imports protecting locals at consumer cost.
- Quotas cap quantities; subsidies boost chosen exporters instead.
- Trade deficits mirror capital surpluses under balance-of-payments math.
- Supply chains splinter production across comparative-advantage nodes.
- Currency moves act as across-the-board tariff/subsidy shifts.
- Trade deals (USMCA, EU single market) cut barriers among members.
- Infant-industry protection claims need sunset clauses to avoid cronyism.
- Services trade (finance, IT) now rivals goods flows digitally.
- Decoupling/reshoring debates weigh resilience against efficiency.
