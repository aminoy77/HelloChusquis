# Recessions (example)

- Recessions broadly mean shrinking output plus rising joblessness.
- NBER dates US cycles by depth, diffusion, and duration, not two-quarter rules.
- Inverted yield curves have predicted most postwar US recessions.
- Automatic stabilizers (unemployment insurance) cushion without new votes.
- Stimulus (spending, rate cuts) fights demand shortfalls relatively fast.
- Austerity during slumps deepens holes by cutting demand further.
- Jobless recoveries restore output before employment repeatedly.
- Balance-sheet recessions need debt write-downs, not just cheap credit.
- Leading indicators (claims, orders, sentiment) turn before GDP prints.
- Expansions die from overheating, shocks, or policy mistakes — old age never.
