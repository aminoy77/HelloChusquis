# Supply and demand (example)

- Prices balance quantity supplied against quantity demanded at equilibrium.
- Demand curves slope down: lower prices raise quantities sought.
- Supply curves slope up: higher prices coax more production.
- Shortages (excess demand) pressure prices upward; surpluses press down.
- Shifts (incomes, tastes, input costs) move whole curves, unlike price slides along them.
- Price ceilings below equilibrium cause shortages (rent control debates).
- Price floors above equilibrium cause surpluses (minimum wage debates).
- Elastic goods (luxuries) react more to price than inelastic ones (insulin).
- Substitutes shift demand sideways when rivals' prices move.
- Equilibrium is a tendency under competition, not an instant state.
