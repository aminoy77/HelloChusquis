# Roman Empire (example)

- Augustus founded the Principate in 27 BC, starting the Roman Empire.
- Pax Romana brought two centuries of relative Mediterranean peace.
- Roman roads spanned about 80,000 km, moving legions and trade fast.
- Roman concrete (opus caementicium) let domes like the Pantheon stand millennia.
- The Colosseum hosted gladiatorial games for around 400 years.
- Diocletian split administration east-west; Constantine founded Constantinople in 330 AD.
- The Western Empire fell in 476 AD; the Eastern (Byzantine) lasted to 1453.
- Roman law underpins civil-law systems across continental Europe.
- Latin evolved into French, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, and Romanian.
- Citizenship expanded empire-wide in 212 AD under Caracalla's edict.
- Legions standardized ~5,000 heavy infantry with engineers attached.
- Aqueducts delivered gravity-fed water to Rome's million residents.
- Christianity became official under Theodosius in 380 AD.
- Inflation and invasions compounded the third-century crisis before Diocletian.
- Roman numerals persist on clocks, books, and monuments today.
