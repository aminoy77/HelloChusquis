# Opium Wars (example)

- Britain forced opium into China, then warred over seizures (1839-42, 1856-60).
- Nanjing Treaty (1842) ceded Hong Kong plus opened treaty ports.
- Second war's Summer Palace burning (1860) humiliated Qing power visibly.
- Taiping Rebellion overlapped, compounding Qing crises catastrophically.
- Extraterritoriality exempted foreigners from Chinese law insultingly.
- Missionaries plus merchants penetrated interior footholds steadily.
- Silver outflows reversed as opium bills drained Qing treasuries.
- Century-of-humiliation narratives still shape Chinese statecraft.
- Boxer Rebellion (1900) continued anti-foreign backlash violently.
- Unequal treaties ended only amid WWII and revolution.
