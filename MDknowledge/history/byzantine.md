# Byzantine Empire (example)

- Byzantium centered on Constantinople from 330 to 1453 AD.
- Justinian's code (529-534) preserved Roman law for Europe's future.
- Hagia Sophia's dome (537) awed visitors for a thousand years.
- Greek fire (naval flamethrowers) saved Constantinople repeatedly.
- Themes (military provinces) funded defense through soldier-farmers.
- Iconoclasm (726-843) convulsed church and state over holy images.
- The 1054 schism split Catholic West from Orthodox East formally.
- Crusader sack of 1204 crippled Byzantium more than any Muslim siege.
- Constantine XI died fighting when Mehmed II took the city in 1453.
- Byzantine scholars fleeing westward fueled Renaissance Greek revival.
- Cyril and Methodius gave Slavs liturgy and the Glagolitic script.
- Hippodrome chariot factions (Blues/Greens) doubled as political mobs.
- Silk smuggling (monks, silkworm eggs) broke Persia's monopoly.
- Varangian guards (Norse, English) served emperors as elite axes.
- Eastern Orthodoxy still crowns Moscow's self-image as Third Rome.
