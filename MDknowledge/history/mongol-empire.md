# Mongol Empire (example)

- Genghis Khan united the steppe tribes in 1206 and built history's largest land empire.
- Mongol horse archers combined mobility, discipline, and composite bows.
- The Yam relay network moved messages across Eurasia in days.
- Pax Mongolica secured Silk Road trade between Europe and China.
- Kublai Khan founded China's Yuan dynasty and hosted Marco Polo.
- Karajorum served as the early imperial capital before Khanbaliq (Beijing).
- Siege engineers (Chinese, Persian) cracked walled cities resisting cavalry.
- The empire split into Golden Horde, Ilkhanate, Chagatai, and Yuan realms.
- Plague likely rode Mongol trade arteries westward in the 1340s.
- Tamerlane's later conquests echoed Mongol methods without lasting unity.
- Religious tolerance attracted useful administrators of all faiths.
- Mongol law (Yassa) standardized order across conquered peoples.
- Baghdad fell to Hulegu in 1258, ending the Abbasid caliphate.
- Naval invasions of Japan failed twice amid famous kamikaze storms.
- Modern Mongolia still honors Genghis as founding father.
