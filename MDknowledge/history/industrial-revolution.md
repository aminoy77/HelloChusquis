# Industrial Revolution (example)

- The Industrial Revolution began in 18th-century Britain with mechanized textiles.
- Watt's steam engine (1776) powered factories, mines, and railways.
- Railways collapsed travel times and unified national markets in the 1800s.
- Urbanization crowded workers into fast-growing industrial cities.
- The factory system replaced cottage craft with wage labor and shifts.
- Steel (Bessemer process) and coal underpinned bridges, rails, and ships.
- Child labor and 12-hour days eventually triggered reform movements.
- Trade unions and strikes won shorter hours and safer conditions.
- Steamships and telegraphs globalized trade and news in the 19th century.
- Germany and the USA industrialized next, shifting the world's economic center.
